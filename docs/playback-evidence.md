# Completed playback evidence

Players submit device-reported completed segments to `POST /api/device/play-events` with their device Bearer credential. Console previews and heartbeats never submit playback events. These are device claims about rendered playback, not camera evidence, human exposure, billable impressions or causal sales lift.

Request: `{ "events": [{ "eventId": "UUID", "mediaId": "media-id", "programId": null, "startAt": 1760000000000, "endAt": 1760000015000, "durationMs": 15000 }] }`. Program playback uses its manifest ID; direct video uses null. At most 50 events per batch. Response: `{ "accepted": ["UUID"], "rejected": [{ "eventId": "UUID", "reason": "binding_changed" }] }`. Both arrays explicitly acknowledge processing. Transport/server failures retain queued entries. Rejection reasons include invalid/expired timestamps, obsolete binding, unauthorized segments and overlapping segments.

The collector derives tenant/device/store/group from the authenticated current binding, locks that device row during collection, ignores caller-supplied scope, and rejects events beginning before the binding changed. Events must finish within the bounded server time range, be at most seven days old, and have duration 1 second–1 hour. Program events must match an authorized current/historical deployment's exact playlist segment boundaries; direct-video duration must match media metadata. Unique IDs deduplicate retries; overlapping intervals for the same device are also rejected. A heartbeat saying “playing” does not create an event.

Browser, macOS and Android track at the playback tick, observing before and after schedule changes. Tracking is armed only when the segment start is observed exactly or a continuous healthy sample crosses into a new segment within 150 ms and requires render readiness throughout, no sampling gap over 250 ms, end-boundary coverage, and a continuous control/program identity. Pause, seeking, buffering, control changes, partial joins and missed segments discard the candidate. Repeated adjacent entries using the same asset remain separate segments. Video completion additionally requires an observed actual playback-position wrap near the segment boundary, not merely elapsed schedule time; trimmed or non-loop-aligned video segments can therefore be conservatively omitted. Direct still images have no defined completion and are excluded; images in a timed playlist can complete. Conservative omission is preferred to inventing a full play. These checks sample player state and cannot prove every frame was physically displayed.

Each credential binding has a local persistent queue, capped at 500 events and seven days, with batches of 50 retried roughly every ten seconds. Oldest records are discarded at the cap; this is not an exhaustive long-term offline ledger. Browser persistence depends on permitted localStorage; unavailable/cleared storage, OS termination before persistence or device reset can lose evidence. Stored payloads contain no device keys. Rebinding makes older queued events ineligible at the server. Duplicate acknowledgments are safe.

A wall with several screens yields several **device completions**. Reporting must distinguish that raw total from a group-level occurrence deduplicated by group/program/media/segment time, and from estimated impressions. No audience multiplier is inferred by the player.

Validation uses temporary PostgreSQL tenant-isolation fixtures, JS/Swift/Android timeline checks, and a persistent Android queue retry fixture. Local tests/builds do not establish physical TV behavior, field delivery completeness or an audited billing system. macOS 0.5.0 is ad-hoc signed; the public-checkout Android APK uses its existing separate signing identity and cannot replace an installation signed with a different private key.
